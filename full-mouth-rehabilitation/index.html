Full-mouth rehabilitation · Circular Quay

Rebuilding worn teeth and a stable bite

When teeth have changed over time, treatment begins with understanding why.

Teeth can become shorter, thinner, chipped or sensitive as a result of wear, damage and changes in the way they function.

Full-mouth rehabilitation involves assessing and, where appropriate, restoring multiple teeth to address structural, functional and aesthetic concerns.

Dr Aodhan Docherty plans restorative treatment around the condition of the teeth, the relationship between the upper and lower jaws and the factors contributing to tooth wear.

When might full-mouth rehabilitation be considered?

Some patients notice that their teeth have gradually become shorter or flatter. Others experience repeated chipping, worn fillings, sensitivity or difficulty chewing.

A comprehensive assessment may be appropriate when there are multiple concerns affecting the teeth or bite.

Full-mouth rehabilitation does not necessarily mean that every tooth requires treatment. The extent of treatment depends on the clinical findings and the patient's needs.

Why do teeth wear down?

Tooth wear can develop for several reasons, sometimes involving more than one factor.

Grinding and clenching

Repeated or excessive forces can contribute to wear, fractures and damage to existing restorations.

Acid erosion

Dietary acids and stomach acid can gradually dissolve tooth enamel, changing the shape and surface of teeth.

Mechanical wear

Tooth-to-tooth contact and other mechanical factors can contribute to the loss of tooth structure.

Existing dental restorations

Old or failing restorations, missing teeth and changes in the bite may influence how forces are distributed.

Identifying the contributing factors is important because restoring the teeth without addressing ongoing causes may increase the risk of further damage.

A detailed assessment of your teeth and bite

Planning begins with an examination of the teeth, gums, existing restorations and the way the upper and lower teeth meet.

Depending on the case, this may include:

  • Clinical photographs and digital scans
  • Assessment of tooth wear and remaining tooth structure
  • Evaluation of the bite and jaw movements
  • Assessment of the jaw joints and associated muscles
  • Appropriate diagnostic imaging

The findings help determine whether treatment is needed, which teeth require attention and what options may be appropriate.

Planning the proposed changes

Complex restorative treatment often involves more planning than replacing an individual filling or crown.

A diagnostic wax-up or digital design may be used to explore proposed changes to tooth shape, height and position.

In suitable cases, a trial restoration may help assess aspects of the proposed design before definitive treatment.

Treatment may also be divided into stages, allowing particular dental concerns to be addressed before others.

Treatment options

The recommended approach depends on the amount of remaining tooth structure, the condition of existing restorations and the functional demands on the teeth.

Composite resin

Composite may be used to rebuild worn or chipped areas of teeth where clinically appropriate.

Porcelain restorations

Porcelain veneers, onlays or crowns may be considered when teeth require restoration of their shape, structure or appearance.

Dental crowns

Crowns may be recommended for teeth with significant structural damage or existing large restorations.

Occlusal splints

A custom-made splint may be recommended for some patients, particularly where grinding or clenching is a concern.

Different teeth may require different treatments within the same overall plan.

How long does full-mouth rehabilitation take?

The number of appointments and overall timeline depend on the complexity of treatment.

Some cases can be managed in a limited number of restorative appointments. Others require diagnostic planning, provisional restorations, staged treatment and reviews over a longer period.

Aodhan will explain the proposed sequence and estimated timeframe after completing an assessment.
